# Niche: casual games and companion apps

**Preset:** D3 · E5 · W5 — saturated, springy and playful. The most expressive niche, with character in every corner.
**Stakes:** trust 3/5 (in-app spending, children playing, randomised rewards). Manipulative monetisation is the main risk.
**Session:** short bursts of a few minutes, repeated through the day. Companion apps (deck builders, collection trackers) are longer and more data-heavy.

## The user in the moment

Looking for delight, a quick win, or a way to manage a collection. In a game they want to feel the game respond. In a companion tool they want fast lookup and clear numbers. A large share of players are young, so spending controls matter.

## Structure

- **Navigation:** a hub with a few modes. The game owns its own interface during play; the hub stays simple.
- **First screen answers:** "What can I play or do right now?" Resume, pick a mode, or see what changed in the collection.
- **Key screens:** home or mode select · core loop · rewards · store · collection or companion view.
- **Core loop:** controls are immediate and forgiving; feedback is instant. Menus never interrupt play for more than a moment.
- **Collection and companion screens (D4):** dense grids with filters and counts. Keep them readable even when the rest of the app is flamboyant.
- **Sensitive actions:** every purchase shows the real money price and needs a confirmation. Spending limits and parental controls are easy to find.

## Look and feel

- **Cliché to avoid:** casino-style reward bursts and loot-box glow.
- **Over-correction to avoid:** a flat, corporate menu layer on top of a lively game, where buttons are tiny grey rectangles and the store looks like a settings page, which breaks the mood and hides what is being sold.
- **Colour:** characterful and saturated. Use contrast carefully so text remains readable on busy art.
- **Type:** expressive display type is allowed for titles and rewards. Body and price text stays plain and legible.
- **Shape:** big, round and playful.
- **Motion and haptics:** high energy by design. Haptics are part of gameplay (a musical game won an award for its haptic use). Offer a way to reduce motion and effects.

## Trust signals

- Clear odds whenever a reward is random.
- Real money prices shown wherever a currency is spent.
- Spending limits and parental controls.
- A plain explanation of what a purchase gives.

## States you must design

Offline play · save and resume · purchase confirmation and purchase failure · anti-binge reminder · low battery · reward pending · event ended · collection empty · connection lost mid-match · age or parental restriction.

## Refuse to build

Pay-to-continue pressure on children; hidden currencies that obscure the real price; fake scarcity timers; randomised rewards without disclosed odds; characters that guilt the player into buying; reward bursts on every tap.

## Learn from

- **Pokemon TCG Pocket** — Apple iPhone Game of the Year 2025, praised for an iPhone-friendly interface. Borrow the collection browsing built for one hand. Check its randomised rewards against the odds rule above before copying any mechanic.
- **Balatro** — Apple Design Award 2025. Borrow how strong visual identity and clear feedback carry a simple loop.
- **Sago Mini Jinja's Garden** — Apple Design Award 2026 for Interaction. Borrow effortless, forgiving controls.
- **Pine Hearts** — Apple Design Award 2026 for Inclusivity. Borrow its accessibility options for legibility, controls, motion and sensory feedback.

## What users complain about here

No game-specific complaint data was retrieved. The cross-niche complaints that clearly apply: fake urgency and scarcity timers; gamification pressure and loss framing; paywalls placed before fun; and notification requests on first launch.

## Accessibility specifics

- Options for text size and legibility, control remapping or simplification, reduced motion and reduced sensory feedback.
- Never rely on colour alone for game state or rarity; add shape or label.
- Offer audio and haptic alternatives for key cues.
- Timed actions have an option to slow or remove the timer.
- Collection screens work with a screen reader and large text.
