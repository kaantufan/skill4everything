# Niche: dating

**Preset:** D2 · E3 · W4 — image-led cards, gentle motion, warm and round.
**Stakes:** trust 4/5 (personal safety, location, intimate photos and messages). Mistakes expose people.
**Session:** short, repeated visits of a few minutes, often in idle moments, with strong feelings attached.

## The user in the moment

Hopeful and a little anxious. They judge people quickly from photos and a few lines, and worry about safety, about being ghosted, and about wasting time. Fatigue is common. Anything that feels like a trick to sell upgrades makes an already tense experience worse.

## Structure

- **Navigation:** 4 tabs (for example Discover, Likes, Chats, Profile). Swiping is optional; always provide buttons for like and pass.
- **First screen answers:** "Who is here for me to look at, and did anyone respond?" The next profile card is the screen. Counters and teasers stay out of the way.
- **Key screens:** profile builder (prompts and photos) · discovery card · match moment · chat · safety tools · subscription page.
- **Discovery card:** photo-led, with a few lines of text and a way to open the full profile. Text over photos always sits on a scrim.
- **Sensitive actions:** report, unmatch and block are one tap from any profile or chat. Sharing location or contact details is an explicit choice with control over precision.

## Look and feel

- **Cliché to avoid:** red-orange flame and heart gradients, and swipe left and right drawn as a literal card toss.
- **Over-correction to avoid:** a sterile, text-heavy profile list in neutral grey that treats people as database rows and removes all warmth from meeting someone.
- **Colour:** warm and friendly, but with enough contrast over photos. Use a scrim under every caption. Keep the accent for likes and primary actions.
- **Type:** friendly, readable, with room for personality in prompt answers. Keep names and key facts prominent.
- **Shape:** round cards and buttons, large photo areas.
- **Motion and haptics:** playful but not casino-like. A gentle match animation and a light haptic on like and match. Avoid slot-machine variable rewards and flashing "likes waiting" teasers.

## Trust signals

- Visible verification badges and photo verification.
- Report and unmatch are always available and always one tap.
- Clear controls for how precisely location is shared.
- Explicit consent before sharing photos or contact details, and clear messaging about what is free and what is paid.

## States you must design

No more profiles nearby (honest copy, not a fake "come back later" trick) · expired match · message failed · location off · blocked user · unmatched or ghosted · photo failed verification · account under review · empty likes · no connection.

## Refuse to build

Paywalling basic filters; price discrimination without disclosure; hidden "rose jail" carousels of locked profiles; fake "likes waiting" teasers designed to force a purchase; engagement mechanics that mimic a slot machine.

## Learn from

- **Benchmark evidence only:** a 2024 usability benchmark ranked major dating apps poorly, with Tinder near the bottom. Treat the whole category as a low bar you can beat with clarity and honest copy.
- **Commonly cited: Hinge** — profile prompts that give people something to talk about. Borrow the idea of prompt-led profiles.
- **Commonly cited: Bumble** — design efforts aimed at reducing swipe fatigue. The detail is unverified, so treat it as a direction, not a model.

## Details leading apps get right

- Likes attach to specific content. Hinge reported in 2024 that likes on text prompts were 47% more likely to lead to a date than likes on photos.
- The structure follows the premise: shared interests sit right under the photo, shared items are checked and the rest greyed, and each prompt can carry a tag for what you share.
- Pass and Like are a large, labelled, pinned bar that never covers content. Floating buttons over the first prompt cut its text.
- A like gives feedback: the next profile appears with a "Liked: name / Undo" toast, undo over confirm.
- The header collapses to name, age and a verified badge on scroll.
- Report and block appear once, at the end of the profile, not beside the like zone.
- Copy is localised to the app's language, and photos are real or convincing; grey boxes cost the most in blind judging.

## What users complain about here

Paywalls everywhere: paid tiers on Tinder, Bumble and Hinge hide features that used to be basic, and locked "standouts" carousels; swipe fatigue (a poll reported secondhand put it at a large majority of young adults, so treat the figure as indicative only); forced accounts and permissions before value.

## Accessibility specifics

- Photos and prompts have alt text or a text description.
- Swipe has button alternatives at the same size and prominence.
- Text over images always has a scrim so it stays readable for low-vision users.
- Match, like and verification states are conveyed by text or icon, not colour alone.
- Chat respects large text sizes without cutting off message text.
