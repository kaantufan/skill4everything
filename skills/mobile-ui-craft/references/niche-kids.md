# Niche: kids

**Preset:** D1 · E4 · W5 — very few choices, responsive and lively, large and round.
**Stakes:** trust 5/5 (children, parents' money, privacy law). Two audiences with different needs: the child plays, the parent decides and pays.
**Session:** a child plays for 5–30 minutes; a parent visits briefly to set up, pay or check.

## The user in the moment

The child cannot read well, taps without hesitation, and does not understand money or consent. The parent is cautious, often in the room, and wants to know what the child sees, who can reach them, and what it costs. Design for both, and keep the two areas clearly separate.

## Structure

- **Navigation (child):** minimal, icon-first, with no dependence on reading. Very few choices on screen at once.
- **Navigation (parent):** a separate, gated area. A parent gate guards settings, purchases and external links.
- **First screen answers (child):** "What can I play or do?" A handful of large, recognisable choices.
- **Key screens:** child home · activity · parent gate · parent dashboard · subscription (parent-only).
- **Child area:** no ads, no purchase buttons, no links out, no chat with strangers. Content is curated rather than an open feed.
- **Sensitive actions:** purchases and account changes sit behind the parent gate. A gate question is hard for a young child but easy for an adult, and it does not rely on reading ability alone.

## Look and feel

- **Cliché to avoid:** a primary-colour rainbow with comic-style fonts.
- **Over-correction to avoid:** a muted, minimalist adult style with tiny controls and small text, which a child cannot use and which looks unwelcoming.
- **Colour:** bright but not harsh. Avoid colour that tires young eyes. Strong shape contrast so objects are recognisable without relying on hue.
- **Type:** large, rounded and clear. Pair text with icons and audio.
- **Shape:** big round shapes, generous spacing, large targets well above what adults need.
- **Motion and haptics:** responsive and forgiving. Taps and swipes react at once. Effortless swipe-to-move was praised in an award-winning garden game for young children. Motion is rich but can be turned down.

## Trust signals

- A clear parental gate and a parent dashboard that shows what the child did.
- No behavioural advertising and no hidden purchases.
- Privacy rules are followed. The updated COPPA rule broadens the definition of personal information and consent expectations; it became final in January 2025, took effect in June 2025, and requires compliance by April 2026.
- Pricing is shown to the parent only, with the renewal date.

## States you must design

Offline play · locked or paid content with a calm redirect to the parent · time limit reached (calm, not scary) · content moderation fallback · parent gate failed · no content downloaded · app interrupted mid-activity · low battery · volume off.

## Refuse to build

Advertising in the child area; in-app purchases a child can reach; manipulative rewards or characters begging for a purchase; social contact with strangers; sad or scolding characters when a child does not buy; fine gestures that young children cannot do.

## Learn from

- **Sago Mini Jinja's Garden** — Apple Design Award 2026 for Interaction. Borrow the effortless swipe-to-move and forgiving controls.
- **ABCmouse 2** — Google Play Best for Families 2025. Study its family-oriented structure; the research gives no further detail.
- **A solo developer's kids audio app** — a developer's public lessons: keep a separate child player and parent admin, curate browsing, and guide onboarding, which improved retention.

## What users complain about here

Price pushback: an annual subscription felt steep to some parents. From the wider evidence: paywalls that arrive before value, and hidden renewals. Child-specific complaint data was not gathered.

## Accessibility specifics

- Audio prompts for every instruction; no reliance on reading.
- Touch targets larger than the adult minimum, with no tiny or precise gestures.
- Shapes and icons carry meaning, not only colour.
- Offer a reduced-motion and quieter-sound option.
- The parent area meets the same text-size and screen-reader needs as any adult app.
