# Niche: wellness, meditation, mental health

**Preset:** D1 · E1 · W4 — one thing per screen, very quiet, soft and warm.
**Stakes:** trust 4/5 (vulnerable users, mood data, health claims). A careless message at a bad moment can do harm.
**Session:** a 5–30 minute guided session, often at night, in bed, or when the user is distressed.

## The user in the moment

Tired, stressed, or low. They have little energy for choices. They want to press one button and be calm. Many open the app at night with the screen close to their face. Some are in real distress, so the app must never feel clever, loud or judgemental.

## Structure

- **Navigation:** few tabs (for example Today, Library, Sleep, Profile). If the app makes any clinical or support claims, crisis resources stay reachable from every main screen.
- **First screen answers:** "What should I do right now?" One recommended session, one start button. The library sits one tap away.
- **Key screens:** today recommendation · session player (minimal) · sleep sounds with a timer · check-in or mood log · gentle progress · subscription page.
- **Session player:** title, a calm visual, play and pause, a progress line. Nothing else competes.
- **Sensitive actions:** deleting mood history and cancelling a subscription are clear and honest, never hidden.

## Look and feel

- **Cliché to avoid:** teal and lavender gradients, stock mountain and forest photos, rounded blobs on every surface.
- **Over-correction to avoid:** a stark, empty screen with grey text on white that feels like a settings page and has no warmth or sense of care.
- **Colour:** desaturated, low-glare palettes. Provide a warm dark mode for night use. Avoid pure white screens that glare in a dark room.
- **Type:** a soft serif or rounded sans at generous sizes with ample line spacing. Few weights.
- **Shape:** large radii, soft edges, plenty of space. One focal element per screen.
- **Motion and haptics:** slow, breathing-paced easing, with no sudden sounds or flashes. Gentle haptic taps may follow a breath cue. Honour the system reduce-motion setting fully.

## Trust signals

- State clearly if clinicians reviewed content, and make no clinical claim that is not true.
- Say that mood and journal data is private and not sold.
- Pricing is transparent: trial end date and renewal price before purchase.
- Crisis support contacts are easy to find and not buried in settings.

## States you must design

Offline with downloaded sessions · interrupted session that can resume · sleep timer ended (fade out, no abrupt stop) · a week with no data (no shaming) · low connectivity · paused or expired subscription · first-time user with no history · content load failure with a calm retry.

## Refuse to build

Streak guilt and "you broke your streak" pushes; cancel-maze billing; a free tier cut down to almost nothing; fake therapist or clinical claims; loud promotional interruptions between sessions; celebratory effects after meditation; confirmshaming copy on cancel.

## Learn from

- **Gentler Streak** — Apple Design Award winner whose gentle tone avoids guilt. Borrow the voice, not the fitness content.
- **Finch** — a gentle pet companion that makes self-care feel kind, per a design critique. Do not copy how paid limits restrict care.
- **Calm** — recognised by Google Play in 2025 for its XR headset app. Borrow its sense of space. Do not copy the billing and cancellation friction users report.

## Details leading apps get right

- A night palette: near-black, a warm accent, low brightness. A light theme at 11 pm reads as glare. Calm's own writing says dimmer warm tones are less likely to interfere with melatonin, and that brightness and timing matter more than hue.
- The whole bedtime job fits on one screen with no scroll: a countdown to lights out, one big start button, sound tiles with a selected or Off state, last night's log and "I'm going to sleep". Stacking more features overwhelms people trying to relax.
- The session itself is calm and finger-sized: a big countdown ring ("Breathe out 6"), elapsed and total time, a large pause, End and a sound toggle.
- Times use one format throughout, with AM or PM or 24 hour consistently, and a sparkline carries day labels.
- A timeline shows "Now" and lights-out without the markers overlapping.
- An alarm line gives context ("06:45, in 7 h 40 min").
- The primary button has high contrast without a blue-white blast, and secondary grey text stays readable.

## What users complain about here

Cancellation friction and confusing billing; unexpected annual renewals; free content shrinking as the product matures, so users feel they can barely try it before the paywall. These come from review-analysis sources, so describe them as patterns, not exact rates.

## Accessibility specifics

- Audio-first design: every session works fully with a screen reader and with the screen off.
- Offer captions or transcripts for guided audio.
- Dim the screen and reduce contrast sensibly for sleep, but keep controls findable.
- No flashing or fast motion. Keep tap targets large and forgiving for low-energy states.
- Offer a visible, simple way to stop or lower the volume at any time.
