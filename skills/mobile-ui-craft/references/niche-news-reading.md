# Niche: news and reading

**Preset:** D4 · E1 · W1 — a dense, scannable feed, no motion, formal and sharp. The article reader drops to D1.
**Stakes:** trust 4/5 (credibility of sources, honest labelling, honest pricing). Calm and trust are the product.
**Session:** a short scan of headlines, then a deep read of one or two articles that can take several minutes.

## The user in the moment

Wants to know what happened without being manipulated. They scan fast, pick a few items, and expect to read them without interruption. They are annoyed by tapping a headline and then hitting a paywall they did not see coming, or by ads that shift the text as they read.

## Structure

- **Navigation:** 3–5 tabs (for example Today, Following or Sections, Saved, Search). The article reader is distraction-free.
- **First screen answers:** "What matters today?" A short, ranked list of headlines with source and time. No autoplay video.
- **Key screens:** feed · article (type first) · saved and offline · notification preferences · subscription.
- **Feed:** dense, scannable rows with headline, source, time and a small image. Paywalled items are marked before the tap.
- **Article reader (D1):** one column, comfortable line length, generous spacing, reading position remembered, nothing floating over the text.
- **Sensitive actions:** subscription and cancellation are clear and match sign-up in effort.

## Look and feel

- **Cliché to avoid:** red breaking-news banners on everything, and autoplay video.
- **Over-correction to avoid:** an over-designed magazine layout with decorative typography and full-bleed images that makes the feed slow to scan and the text hard to read.
- **Colour:** neutral, high contrast, low saturation. Offer light, dark and a sepia-style reading theme. Reserve a single strong colour for genuinely urgent alerts.
- **Type:** a high-quality serif or sans for reading, with a clear hierarchy from headline to deck to body. The reader is the place for the best type in the product.
- **Shape:** sharp or small radii, thin dividers, almost no cards.
- **Motion and haptics:** minimal. Subtle page transitions only. No attention-seeking animation.

## Trust signals

- Source labels on every item and update timestamps on developing stories.
- Corrections are visible and linked.
- Coverage or bias labels where offered (an award-recognised app is built on this idea).
- Honest notification wording that matches the content.

## States you must design

Paywalled (marked before the tap) · offline cache · updated story (what changed) · notification pause · reading position restore · article failed to load · no saved items · source unavailable · cookie or consent choice that does not cover the content · very long article.

## Refuse to build

A surprise paywall after the tap; notification overload and alarmist push copy; ads between paragraphs that shift the layout; cookie banners that hide the content; interstitials on launch or back; unclear subscription terms about what is included.

## Learn from

- **Ground News** — an Apple Design Award finalist for Social Impact in 2025. Borrow its source and coverage labelling that helps readers judge what they read.
- **Primary: News in Depth** — Apple Design Award 2026 for Social Impact, built for Vision Pro. Borrow its focus on depth; the spatial format does not transfer to phones.
- **Apple News+ (as a caution)** — users report confusion about which articles are included. Show clearly what a subscription covers.

## What users complain about here

The top complaint in review analysis is tapping a headline and then hitting an unflagged paywall; also ad and paywall interruptions in major news apps, and notification volume. These come from vendor review analysis and snippets, so describe them as patterns.

## Accessibility specifics

- Text size follows the system setting through the whole article, and layouts do not break at the largest sizes.
- Offer text-to-speech for articles.
- High-contrast and dark themes are tested on long reading.
- Images have alt text, and videos have captions.
- Headings are marked so a screen-reader user can jump through the article.
